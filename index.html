Fed - Financial Stability Report
Información Pública Periodicidad: semestral Última actualización: 6 May 2026 Fuente: www.federalreserve.gov
Executive Summary
  • ▶Valuations stay elevated, risk premiums edge up: the S&P 500 forward P/E (~21x) remains in the upper range of its history (median 16x) and the equity premium sits near a 20-year low. The Treasury term premium rose to the top of its 15-year range amid rate volatility tied to the Middle East.
  • ▶Private credit is the new stress point: a $1,4 tn market (~10% of nonfinancial corporate debt). Semi-liquid funds faced redemption requests well above 5% of NAV in 2026:Q1; most capped them at 5%, and for the first time redemptions exceeded inflows.
  • ▶Hedge fund leverage at record highs: gross leverage held near ~9,8x, concentrated in the largest funds. A smaller cash-futures basis trade was offset by swap-spread and other relative-value trades. Life insurer leverage remains in the top quartile.
  • ▶Banks and funding remain solid: CET1 ratios are near historical highs and securities fair value losses narrowed to ~$300 bn (from ~$690 bn in 2022). Uninsured deposits are well below 2023 peaks; MMF assets reached $7,9 tn and stablecoins $320 bn.

Key Change (vs. FSR November 2025)
  • ▶From policy uncertainty to a war and oil shock: geopolitical risk is now cited by 75% of market contacts (48% in the fall) and an oil shock by 70% (absent before). Private credit jumped to 50% from 22%, while policy uncertainty fell to 20% from 61%. The Fed's vulnerability ratings did not move.
Near-Term Risks
  • ▶ Protracted Middle East conflict: commodity shortages push up inflation and slow growth; investors pull back from richly valued assets and dollar funding tightens for non-U.S. banks.
  • ▶ Higher term premiums plus persistent inflation: asset price declines, heavier debt service for leveraged firms facing refinancing, and new fair value losses at intermediaries.
  • ▶ Cyberattacks, now with LLMs and agentic AI able to find and exploit vulnerabilities; attacks on critical third-party providers could hit many institutions at once.
  • ▶ Continued private credit redemptions and AI disruption of software borrowers could cut credit to riskier firms; the Fed judges systemic spillovers limited.

Executive Summary
  • ▶Valuations back to elevated after the tariff shock: April's declines largely reversed, the forward P/E returned to the high end of its range (~23x) and the equity premium neared a 20-year low. Corporate spreads settled at tight pre-April levels and Treasury liquidity recovered.
  • ▶Private-sector debt at a two-decade low vs GDP: public firms service debt comfortably, but small businesses and risky privately held firms have weaker debt-servicing capacity. Card and auto delinquencies remain above pre-pandemic levels.
  • ▶Leverage keeps building outside banks: hedge fund leverage rose across Treasury, rate-derivative and equity strategies; life insurers are in the top quartile. Bank credit commitments to NBFIs reached $2,5 tn, led by SPEs, CLOs and ABS.
  • ▶Runnables near pre-GFC size, but safer: runnable liabilities equal ~85% of GDP, close to 2007 levels. Yet the riskiest components are about half their pre-crisis size; growth came from government MMFs ($7,1 tn in July). Stablecoins reached ~$300 bn after the GENIUS Act.

Key Change (vs. FSR April 2025)
  • ▶Tariff shock faded, complacency risk rose: trade risk, the top concern in spring (73%), dropped off the survey's top list. Policy uncertainty led (61%), and AI appeared for the first time (30%) as a possible trigger for a correction.
Near-Term Risks
  • ▶ Higher-than-anticipated long-term rates from rising term premiums and persistent inflation, straining borrowers and causing fair value losses at lenders.
  • ▶ A marked global slowdown triggering a pullback from richly valued assets, with high public debt limiting governments' room to respond.
  • ▶ A turn in AI sentiment, the main driver of U.S. equity gains, could spark a correction with losses in public and private markets.
  • ▶ Cyberattacks and cyber events at financial market utilities or critical third-party providers.

Hedge fund leverage
Mean ratio (x), quarterly

Gross leverage = Gross notional exposure / NAV
Balance sheet leverage = Gross asset value / NAV
Gross notional exposure adds the full notional value of derivatives (futures, swaps, options) to on-balance-sheet assets, while gross asset value only counts the margin posted on them. The gap between the two lines therefore reflects leverage taken through derivatives.
Bank securities fair value gains/losses
Fair value minus amortized cost, billions of dollars, semiannual
Losses narrowed to ~$300 bn from ~$690 bn, still sensitive to long rates.
AFS (available-for-sale): securities banks may sell before maturity. Carried at fair value, so price changes show up in equity.
HTM (held-to-maturity): securities banks intend to keep until maturity. Carried at amortized cost, so losses are not recognized unless the bank is forced to sell.
Bars = Fair value − Amortized cost of both portfolios combined. When rates rise, bond prices fall and losses widen: they remain a hidden risk if a bank has to sell, as happened with SVB in 2023.
